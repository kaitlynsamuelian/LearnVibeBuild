# Morning briefing — the wide hunt

Hey. So you asked for the big swing — go past your five ideas and actually hunt for
what else could be out there. I spent the last stretch going wide across basically
every domain you're into, plus deliberately hunting the dumb little "ugh" moments
everyone just accepts (umbrellas, shared fridges, laundry rooms, the works), and then
actually validating the promising ones instead of just vibing on them. Full receipts
are in [[capstone-opportunities]]. Here's the honest gut read.

I ended up with 8 real opportunities plus a handful I'm telling you to just skip, and
I'm going to lead with my favorite because I don't want to bury it.

**The one I'd chase first:** a system that helps you understand *why* your days go
the way they go — not another planner, not another app that blocks your phone and
guilt-trips you. Something retrospective. You log a little, it looks at patterns, and
instead of "you procrastinated again," it tells you something closer to "your last
four bad days all started with a bad night's sleep" or "you keep losing momentum
around 3pm specifically." I looked — Lockin, Forest, all of them intervene *in the
moment*. Nobody's building the mirror. And here's the thing that actually got me: this
is basically the tool you've been quietly trying to be for yourself all semester with
the [[procrastination]] and [[nervous-system-regulation]] stuff. That's not a
coincidence I'd ignore. It's software-only, it's deep (real behavioral-data
work, maybe some light pattern modeling), and there's genuinely nobody else doing it
this way yet.

**Second favorite, and the one with the best "whoa" factor for Expo:** a wearable
that translates color into sound or touch, built around colorblind users — think
checking if your shirt and pants clash without pulling out your phone. The software
version of this already exists (an app called WearWithAll does almost exactly this
with your camera), but every single competitor makes you actively scan things. Nobody
has built the passive, always-on, wearable version — something you just wear and it
tells you. That's a real gap, ~8% of men are colorblind so you've got an actual,
easy-to-reach group of people to test with, and it hits your physical prototyping +
electronics + sensors growth goals hard while still being genuinely useful and
accessible. I really like this one.

**Third, with an asterisk I want to be honest about:** a private, on-device app that
correlates your cycle with mood, sleep, and workout performance — basically "why do I
actually feel like this." The need here is enormous and well-documented — Flo has 40
million users AND a $59.5 million lawsuit settlement for selling data to Meta, Clue's
symptom tracking is "broad but shallow" by its own reviewers' admission, and nearly
40% of people quit these apps citing bad or shallow insights. That's about as
validated as a problem gets. The asterisk: this is a personal-health topic, and
whether you'd want to stand at Expo and talk publicly about a period-tracking app is a
values call only you can make, not a technical one. If yes, this is genuinely strong.
If it makes you wince even a little, that's useful information too — trust that.

**Fourth: a lift-form coach that gives you feedback through touch, not a screen** —
so you're not propping your phone up filming yourself mid-set in a crowded gym. I have
to be straight with you though: this is the single most crowded space I found this
whole hunt. There are already at least four AI form-check apps live right now (Form
Fix, CueForm, OptiLiftAI, Gymscore), all doing camera-based pose analysis. The
no-screen, no-camera angle is a real difference, but you'd need to hold that line hard
or it just reads as "another one of those" at Expo.

A close honorable mention: a crowdsourced laundry-availability app for buildings that
will *never* get official smart laundry — which is most college rentals, including
probably yours. It's real and personal, but the sensor-on-every-machine version of
this has literally been done as a class assignment before (found a UIUC project doing
almost exactly that), so it only stays fresh if you commit to the "no landlord buy-in
required" angle instead of defaulting to hardware.

I also chased a few more — an untaggable-object tracker for the stuff AirTags can't
handle (thin, washable, no-battery), a "true size" memory tool for cross-brand
clothes shopping, personalized sound alerts for Deaf/HoH users — full writeups are in
the note if you want them, but I'm being upfront that each has a real catch (weak
demand signal, crowded VC-funded competitors, or a personal-connection gap for you
specifically).

And I want to be honest about what I ruled OUT, because you should know the hunt was
real: wind-resistant umbrellas are already well-solved (Senz, Blunt, and GustBuster
are all genuinely good), food-waste pantry apps are crowded, clothing-swap apps for
college friend groups already exist — built by college students, even, live at
UChicago and NYU — and the "what do my apps know about me" privacy dashboard is
already built into your phone's settings. Good instincts on all of them, just already
covered.

So — my honest top pick is the behavior-insight system, with the color-sense wearable
right behind it as the one with the most "proud to demo this" energy. But I wrote all
eight up in real detail because I don't want to make this call for you.

Which one pulls at you when you actually picture spending a semester building it —
not which one *sounds* smartest, but which one you'd still be excited about in
February when it's 11pm and something's broken?

— Skylar
