# Morning briefing — all five capstone ideas

Hey. You were asleep (or at least away from your laptop) so I went and actually dug
into all five capstone ideas last night — reviews, forums, real prices, the whole
thing. Full receipts are in [[capstone-research-log]], but here's the honest gut read
on each one, idea by idea.

**[[idea-drying-rack]]** — okay so, this category is already *good*. Racks like this
sell for $20-40, get 4.6-star reviews, people genuinely like them once they're up. The
one real crack in the market: every rack rated for actual wet-laundry weight assumes
you're drilling into studs, and the true no-damage options (Command strips, adhesive
rails) can't hold nearly as much. That's a real, scoped mechanical problem you could
solve. But I'd be lying if I said this felt like the deepest option — it's solid and
very "you," but it's improving something that already works pretty well, not fixing
something broken.

**[[idea-speaker-bridge]]** — this one's technically the sexiest and also the one that
worried me most. The easy version of this idea (bridge one old speaker into your phone)
is already a $149 solved product called WiiM Pro, plus $20 Bluetooth dongles that do a
rougher version. The problem that's *actually* still unsolved — getting a Sonos, a
Bluetooth speaker, and a Chromecast speaker all playing in sync together — is a real
hard networking/clock-drift problem, but that's PhD-adjacent hard, not semester-project
hard. If you want this one, we need to be honest about which version you're actually
building before you commit.

**[[idea-fitness-clarity]]** — I liked this one more after researching it, not less,
which doesn't happen often. There's an actual published study naming "fitness data
overload" as a real four-part problem, and real people online are saying almost
word-for-word what your idea note predicted — Oura sleep data called "a useless
number," a Whoop user saying the stats didn't match how they actually felt. Nobody
owns the "okay but what do I DO today" layer. The risk is scope creep — hooking into
Whoop/Oura/Garmin APIs is a real technical lift, so you'd want to start narrow (one
audience, maybe manual entry first) rather than "clarity for everyone."

**[[idea-body-awareness]]** — gut read: this is your strongest one. Every posture/
breath/stress device I found (Upright GO, Spire, Breathing Buddha) just buzzes at you
— "hey you're slouching" — and then leaves you to fix it yourself. None of them
actually *teach* the regulation skill in real time. That's a real, unclaimed gap, and
it's the one idea that hits all three of your priorities at once: it's personal to
you, it's a real unmet need, and it's got genuine sensor/hardware depth. The only
danger is scope — you already flagged this yourself, and research backs it up: pick
ONE signal (I'd bet on breath) and actually hold that line.

**[[idea-closet-organizer]]** — relatable, personal, real friction — but it's the most
crowded field of the five, and I found something you should know before you get
attached: Google Photos is rolling out its own free AI "Digital Closet" wardrobe
sorter, built right into an app people already have open. That's not a small
competitor. If you go this direction you'd need something Google flatly can't do —
like a physical capture setup, or shifting the whole focus from "cataloging clothes"
to "deciding what to wear," which is the part apps still get wrong.

**My honest ranking right now:** body-awareness first, fitness-clarity close second,
drying-rack as a solid safe third, and speaker-bridge and closet-organizer both need a
sharper angle before I'd trust either of them — one because the real problem might be
too hard, the other because the real problem might already be getting solved for free.

Question for you: does body-awareness actually excite you the way it does on paper, or
were you leaning somewhere else emotionally? And if it's breath — want me to dig
tomorrow into specific sensor options (strap vs. clip vs. camera-based) so you have
something concrete to bring to class?

## Follow-up: I went back and dug deeper on speaker-bridge

Okay so you asked me to sharpen up and actually chase down the speaker-bridge
questions specifically — latency compensation, hardware vs. app, whether it's
semester-doable. Here's what I found.

**[[idea-speaker-bridge]]** — good news first: there's real prior art at basically
your exact scope. A group of engineering students in France built "BlueBox" — a
Raspberry Pi with up to 4 USB Bluetooth dongles, one per speaker, syncing playback
across all of them, controlled from a phone app. That's almost word-for-word your hub
concept, and it exists, and it was students who built it. They even had to build a
dedicated latency-optimization piece, which tells me the hard part is real but
chewable in a semester, not a research-lab problem. I also found Snapcast, an
open-source project that shows the actual sync trick you'd generalize from — it
keeps every speaker's clock synced NTP-style and then nudges playback speed by
literally adding or dropping single audio samples to correct drift. It's built for
software speakers, not raw Bluetooth output, so you'd be adapting the idea, not
copying it wholesale — which is honestly the good, meaty part of the project. And
Auracast, the "real" industry fix — I confirmed it needs new chips on both ends, so
it flatly can't touch the JBL-plus-Bose situation, exactly like you suspected.

The thing that still nags at me: I went looking for people actually complaining
about this — like, ranting on Reddit that they wish their mismatched speakers could
sync — and I mostly came up empty. People seem to just... accept it and buy matching
speakers, or grab a cheap receiver box. That doesn't mean nobody wants it, but it
means you shouldn't assume the need — go ask a few actual people (roommates, friends
with mixed speaker setups) whether they'd genuinely use this before you sink a
semester into it. The technical bones here are honestly some of the strongest of your
five ideas. I just don't want you building something beautiful that nobody was
actually asking for.

Question for you: want me to help you draft 3-4 quick questions you could ask
friends/roommates this week to pressure-test whether this is a real want, not just a
cool build? That'd tell us a lot before you commit.

— Skylar
