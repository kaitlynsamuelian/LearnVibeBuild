# 2026-09-16 — a second, wider hunt

Hey. So you told me not to box myself into the stuff already in your notes, so I didn't.
I went and stood in ten different pairs of shoes for you — a senior getting a scary
phone call, someone cooking one-handed, a teacher juggling six kids' accommodations
between periods, a parent leaving the ER with a stack of paper they can't read, a kid
who's about to melt down and doesn't know it yet. Full writeup with sources is in
[[capstone-opportunities]] (new section at the top, dated today) — this is me just
talking you through what actually pulled at me while I was digging.

## The one I'd bet on: adaptive one-handed kitchen tools

This is the one I keep coming back to. People with arthritis, stroke survivors, people
with limb differences — they already have a pile of individual adaptive gadgets (a
special cutting board, a rocker knife), but nobody's built the *set* that gets someone
through making an actual meal, one-handed, start to finish. It's real, it's a real
population, and it is SO squarely in your wheelhouse — 3D printing, laser cutting, the
whole industrial-design process you specifically said you want more of. And it's a
brand-new audience for you, which I liked — you haven't touched disability-and-motor-
skill territory yet, only color vision and hearing in the first hunt. If CU Boulder has
a disability services office or an OT program, that's your test group, sitting right
there on campus.

## The one with the best "real need" receipts: multilingual aftercare instructions

This one's for people leaving urgent care or the ER. Two facts stacked on top of each
other: patients remember like 10-15% of what they're told at discharge, and 25 million
Americans don't have strong English. Nobody's built the thing that fixes BOTH at once —
plain language AND their actual language, for the take-home instructions specifically,
not just the in-person visit. It'd be a real LLM build — genuinely meaty AI/software
work — and it's about as "solve a real problem" as it gets. My honest hesitation: testing
this for real means either fake patient data or an actual clinic partner, and getting a
clinic to work with a student in one semester is not nothing. Worth asking your
capstone advisor early if that's realistic before you fall in love with it.

## The one that made me smile: a classroom version of your own body-awareness idea

You already want to build a breath sensor that catches rising stress before it
spirals — for yourself. What if the same tech went to a kid with sensory-processing
needs in a classroom, catching the moment before a meltdown instead of after? Every
sensory tool out there right now is passive — a fidget toy someone has to remember to
reach for. Nothing closes the loop with an actual physiological signal. I love this one
because it's not a brand-new topic for you, it's the SAME idea, pointed at someone else's
life instead of your own — and designing for someone whose experience isn't yours is a
different, harder skill than building for yourself. The catch is real: getting actual
access to test with a kid in a classroom is a genuine hurdle (parental consent, school
policy) — you'd probably need a family friend's kid or an after-school program, not a
real classroom, and that's an honest thing to plan around, not a dealbreaker.

## The practical one: a renter's-side maintenance tracker

Every maintenance app out there is sold TO landlords. Nothing exists for the tenant who
just wants to know "how many days does my landlord legally have before I can do
something about this," with a photo-and-date paper trail to back it up. It's the
least technically flashy of my top four, but it's the one you could test on your own
building starting literally this week, with your own roommates. Sometimes the boring
answer is the right one to actually finish.

## What I'd skip, honestly

A few things I dug into weren't worth bringing you — dyslexia reading apps, ADHD
homework apps, fermentation trackers, and knitting row counters are all already
crowded with genuinely good, well-built competitors. I didn't want to hand you a
"cool idea" that's secretly already solved five times over.

None of these are meant to replace the ones from the first hunt — the behavior-insight
