# Idea: Fitness/Health Data Clarity

A capstone idea for [[senior-capstone]]. **Problem:** people trying to improve health/
fitness track workouts, calories, macros, progress, sleep, recovery, etc. — but it's
hard to know what actually *matters* or how to use it. **Vision:** something that cuts
through the noise and tells people what to focus on.

- **Target audience:** gym-goers, college students, young adults, beginner/intermediate
  lifters, people actively trying to improve their fitness.
- **Why it fits her:** personally relevant — ties to her own [[fitness-and-nutrition]]
  interest. Product thinking + possibly data/ML + UX from [[capstone-skills]].
- **Fit vs. [[capstone-goals]]:** real need (priority #2) and something she cares about
  (priority #1). Open question: crowded space (lots of fitness apps) — needs a sharp,
  differentiated angle to stand out and stay understandable (priority #3).

## Open questions for research (see [[capstone-research-log]])
- What do existing fitness/health apps get wrong about "what matters"?
- Is there a hardware/wearable angle, or is this software/UX?
- What's the specific, underserved sub-audience and their real frustration?

## Research so far (2026-09-15)
Full findings: [[capstone-research-log]]. Key finding: this is the one idea where
academic research directly confirms her instinct — a published systematic review names
"data overload" in fitness tracking as a real, four-part problem (cognitive burden,
missing context, bad visualization, fragmented ecosystem). Real users echo it: Oura
sleep data called a "useless number," Whoop stats that "didn't fit my own judgment."
**The gap is the thin, opinionated "what do I actually do today" layer** — nobody owns
it. My take: strong on real-need and personal relevance, but needs a narrow audience
(not "everyone using a wearable") and a scoped MVP (maybe manual entry before real API
integration) to stay understandable and not balloon.
