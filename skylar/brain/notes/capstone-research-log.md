### 2026-09-19 — [[idea-drying-rack]] (correction: Polder Mountain Lock)
- A review of the inspiration-log framing caught an overclaim: "every renter-safe option is too weak for wet laundry." That is not true.
- Precedent: [Polder Mountain Lock Dryer (DRY-9816)](https://polder.com/products/over-the-door-mountain-lock-dryer) — over-the-door hooks (doors ≤ 1.8"), no drill required, patented lock, aluminum, **16 ft of drying space, official 40 lb / 18.1 kg**. Screws included for *optional* wall-mount. ~$35–57 retail.
- 40 lb is real-laundry territory, not delicates-only (unlike BAKON ~20 lb, Black+Decker 11 lb, Brabantia 17 lb). So renter-safe + high-capacity is a solved *door* problem.
- **Reframed gap (keep this):** wall racks that hold a lot still want studs; renter-safe racks that hold a lot take the **door or the floor**. Opportunity = high-capacity + collapsible + renter-safe **without** occupying the floor or taking over a door.
- Add this as inspiration in the log. Do not keep the "every renter-safe option is too weak" sentence.

### 2026-09-19 — [[idea-drying-rack]] (deeper: door-hook vs stud-mount vs adhesive)
- Update to the 09-15 finding: the "no-drill" market did not stay empty — it moved **onto the door**. [BAKON over-door](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/bakon-over-door-hanger-drying-rack-review-37557389) (Apartment Therapy, ~$17/2-pack, ~20 lb each, no tools, door ≤~1.8"). [BLACK+DECKER BD0328 telescopic over-door](https://eeb4.be/products/blackdecker-over-the-door-expandable-telescopic-drying-rack-clothes-organizer-towel-hanger-laundry-collapsible-silver/219290296/) expands to 24", folds to 3.5", **11 lb max**. [Brabantia over-door](https://www.homedepot.com/p/Brabantia-Over-the-Door-15-ft-4-5m-Hanging-Drying-Rack-102769/306374154) is the nicer-designed version at **17 lb** (7.5 kg).
- Stud-mount still owns real capacity: [2026 wall-mount roundups](https://homygear.com/best-wall-mounted-drying-rack/) keep putting ZMBESUP-class racks at **~60 lb** and treating that as the serious option.
- Adhesive ceiling is official, not folk wisdom: [3M Command Large Picture Hanging Strips](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005604172/) = **15 lb for four pairs**, specified for a flat picture, not a lever-arm rack. Command folding hanger is ~7.5 lb / 3.4 kg.

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-speaker-bridge]] (deep dive: latency comp, hardware vs. app, feasibility)
- Existing products / prior art: [Snapcast](https://github.com/snapcast/snapcast) (open source) is the cleanest existing sync engine — each client does continuous NTP-style time sync with the server, then corrects drift by trimming/duplicating individual audio samples (a 48kHz sample is ~0.02ms), landing at sub-0.2ms deviation. Big caveat: Snapcast syncs "smart" software clients over a network (e.g. a Pi running snapclient into an aux cable) — it doesn't stream to stock Bluetooth speakers directly, so it solves the clock-sync math but not the "how do I get audio onto a JBL I don't control the firmware of" problem. On the Bluetooth side itself: standard A2DP is strictly **one source → one sink**; Android's "dual audio" is literally two independent, separately-buffered A2DP connections running in parallel, not synced — which is exactly the laggy hack Kaitlyn already flagged. To drive N speakers from one source you need N separate Bluetooth radios, not N connections off one radio.
- Real hobbyist precedent found: [BlueBox / innotech-mvp](https://github.com/innotech-insa/innotech-mvp), a student project out of INSA (a French engineering school) — a Raspberry Pi hub with up to 4 USB Bluetooth dongles, each paired to its own speaker, controlled from a companion Android app, syncing "up to 4 speakers in bluetooth mode." This is almost exactly Kaitlyn's hub concept, built by students. It ships a dedicated `latency-optimization` folder in the repo, which is direct evidence that even at this exact scope, sync was real, non-trivial engineering work — not a solved footnote. Historical adjacent attempt: [Boombotix "Sync"](https://techcrunch.com/2013/11/07/boombotix-develops-sync-tech-for-concurrent-playback-over-bluetooth-seeks-funding-on-kickstarter/) (2013) raised ~$17K on Kickstarter (and later $4M in VC) for an app syncing playback across multiple phones/speakers over WiFi — different architecture (phone-per-speaker, not a hub), over a decade old, and it ended up as a minor feature of a speaker company rather than a lasting standalone product.
- Auracast reality check: dev-ready chips exist now (Nordic nRF5340, NXP IW612, STMicro STM32WBA) and the Bluetooth SIG publishes real implementation guides — but **both** the transmitter and receiver need this new silicon. This confirms her framing exactly: Auracast is a real, elegant fix, but it does nothing for the JBL + Bose she and her friend already own.
- Common complaints / demand signal: couldn't find an active, ongoing Reddit/forum thread of people asking "how do I sync my JBL and my Bose" as a live complaint — general commentary treats cross-brand sync as an accepted, known limitation people route around (buy matched pairs, use one receiver box) rather than something they're actively agitating for a fix to. That's a soft signal that priority #2 ("real problem enough people care about") still isn't proven — it's assumed, not found.
- The gap / opportunity: the still-genuinely-open build is a Pi-style hub, one Bluetooth dongle per target speaker, running Snapcast-style time-sync + micro-speed correction adapted to Bluetooth's own per-link latency variance (rather than Snapcast's assumption of a shared network clock to software clients). That's a concretely scoped, semester-sized MVP — 2 speakers, "good enough" sync — with real prior art (BlueBox) proving the shape works. The "ideal" version (arbitrary speaker count, tight sync, working across closed ecosystems like Sonos that expose no raw output at all) stays confirmed out of reach for one semester.
- Fit note vs. her goals: this deep dive raises confidence on priority #3 — it's a real, well-documented embedded/networking problem with concrete sub-questions and existing repos to learn from and build past, not just cool tech with no substance. It does *not* move priority #2 — the demand signal is still thin. Feasibility-wise, the minimum-viable framing (2 speakers, 2 dongles, "good enough" sync, à la BlueBox) looks realistic for a semester; the "ideal" cross-ecosystem version does not.
- Sources: [Snapcast GitHub](https://github.com/snapcast/snapcast), [Snapcast README](https://github.com/snapcast/snapcast/blob/master/README.md), [BlueBox / innotech-mvp](https://github.com/innotech-insa/innotech-mvp), [Bluetooth SIG — Auracast for developers](https://www.bluetooth.com/auracast/developers/), [Bluetooth LE Audio/Auracast profile stack — Novel Bits](https://novelbits.io/bluetooth-le-audio-auracast-profiles/), [Boombotix Sync — TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/11/07/boombotix-develops-sync-tech-for-concurrent-playback-over-bluetooth-seeks-funding-on-kickstarter/), [Bluetooth multipoint/A2DP one-sink limitation discussion — Quora](https://www.quora.com/Is-it-possible-with-bluetooth-to-send-two-distinct-sources-to-a-single-audio-output-at-the-same-time-The-reverse-of-a-single-source-device-to-send-audio-to-two-different-speakers-at-the-same-time-seems-to-be)

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-drying-rack]]
- Existing products: this category is mature and cheap. Woolite collapsible wall-mount racks (Home Depot/Walmart, ~$20-40), Rebrilliant collapsible wall rack (Wayfair, 4.6/5 stars), ZMBESUP 31.5" foldable rack rated 60 lb, STEP-UP 40" retractable, Anjuer wall rack (Apartment Therapy called it an "instant closet"). Nearly all are thin-wall aluminum, accordion or fold-flat designs, marketed at 60-80 lb capacity — but that capacity assumes screwing into studs.
- Common complaints: surprisingly few and mostly minor — imprecise hole spacing/mounting hardware confusion (Woolite), thin aluminum feeling cheaper than expected for the brand, one Wayfair reviewer got a unit with pieces broken on arrival. No complaints about the *core concept* — people like these once installed.
- The gap / opportunity: every rack that claims real load capacity (60-80 lb) requires drilling into studs — a non-starter for a lot of renters. The genuinely renter-safe no-drill options (Command strips, adhesive rail systems) cap out around 15-40 lb, well under what a load of wet towels or jeans actually weighs. Nobody's solved "truly damage-free AND rated for real wet-laundry loads." That's a legit, scoped mechanical-engineering problem — better anchoring geometry, load distribution, maybe a hybrid mount.
- Fit note vs. her goals: real everyday problem (✅ priority 2), plays hard into her fabrication/industrial-design strengths (✅ priority 1, tangible "make a real product"). Risk: the *category* itself is already well-solved and well-reviewed at low price points, so unless she nails the no-drill/high-capacity mount specifically, it risks feeling like a reskin rather than a deep problem (priority 3 concern from her own note holds up).
- Sources: [Home Depot Woolite reviews](https://www.homedepot.com/p/reviews/Woolite-24-in-W-x-5-12-in-H-Collapsible-Wall-Mount-Drying-Rack-W-84156/318943061/3), [Wayfair Rebrilliant rack](https://www.wayfair.com/storage-organization/pdp/rebrilliant-collapsible-wall-mounted-drying-rack-w010571622.html), [Apartment Therapy Anjuer review](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/anjuer-wall-mounted-drying-rack-review-37550658), [HookandLoop — command strip weight limits](https://www.hookandloop.com/blog/how-much-weight-can-command-strips-hold), [ZMBESUP listing (Amazon)](https://www.amazon.com/Laundry-Collapsible-Aluminum-Capacity-Template/dp/B0CJ3DBD5T)

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-speaker-bridge]]
- Existing products: AirPlay 2 already unifies a lot of brands (Bose, B&O, Denon, Bluesound, Naim). WiiM Pro ($149) bridges old wired stereos into AirPlay 2, Chromecast, Spotify Connect, and Tidal Connect at once, with a real DAC and low latency — this is basically the product Kaitlyn's idea describes, already shipping and well-reviewed. Cheap Bluetooth adapters ($15-50) do a simpler version. Belkin SoundForm Connect adds AirPlay 2 to any powered speaker.
- Common complaints: mostly protocol-specific bugs (Sonos speakers dropping off Spotify after firmware updates, users frustrated with slow fixes), not "this is impossible" complaints. The deeper frustration in forums is ecosystem fragmentation — Sonos in particular stays closed off from AirPlay/Chromecast multiroom, so a *Sonos + non-Sonos* mixed system still can't play in sync together.
- The gap / opportunity: the "get one old/mismatched speaker talking to your phone" problem is already solved well and cheaply (WiiM Pro, Bluetooth dongles). The real unsolved problem is synchronized multi-speaker playback *across* closed ecosystems (e.g., a Sonos, a Bluetooth speaker, and a Chromecast speaker all playing the same track in sync) — that's a hard clock-drift/buffering/network-sync problem, not a "plug in an adapter" problem.
- Fit note vs. her goals: technically the richest idea for priority 3 (real DSP/networking depth) — but the version of the problem that's actually unsolved (true cross-ecosystem sync) is a big lift for one semester, and the easier version (bridge one old speaker) is already a solved, commodity product. Real risk of building "cool tech" nobody needs because the market already sells the simple fix for $20-150.
- Sources: [WiiM Pro review — AndroidPolice](https://www.androidpolice.com/wiim-pro-review/), [WiiM Pro price/specs — Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marksparrow/2023/03/13/wiim-pro-streamer-offers-great-value-for-bringing-old-hi-fi-up-to-date/), [AirPlay 2 multi-room brands — What Hi-Fi](https://www.whathifi.com/news/apple-announces-airplay-2-multi-room-audio), [Sonos/Spotify connection frustration thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Other-Podcasts-Partners-etc/Spotify-no-longer-connects-to-Sonos-speakers/td-p/4801465)

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-fitness-clarity]]
- Existing products: Whoop, Oura, Garmin dominate; aggregator apps like Cronometer try to pull it all together. A published systematic review actually names this exact problem: "data overload" in fitness tracking, breaking it into four causes — cognitive-attentional burden, contextual relevance deficits, visualization inadequacies, and ecosystem fragmentation.
- Common complaints: Oura sleep-stage data called a "useless number" without deep/REM/light breakdown; Whoop/Oura/Garmin sleep-stage accuracy sits around 51-53% vs. lab measurement; one long-term Whoop user said the stats "often didn't fit my own judgment of how I felt"; cross-app integrations (Oura → Cronometer) reported as flaky/incomplete.
- The gap / opportunity: this is a rare case where the academic literature backs up exactly what Kaitlyn suspected — the problem isn't lack of data, it's translating data into "what do I actually do today," and no major player owns that layer well. Nobody's building the thin, opinionated "here's the one thing that matters this week" layer on top of the raw numbers, especially for a narrow, non-elite audience.
- Fit note vs. her goals: real, validated need (✅ priority 2) and personally relevant to her (✅ priority 1). Biggest risk is scope — building on top of Whoop/Oura/Garmin APIs pulls in real integration complexity, and the space is crowded enough that she'll need a specific, narrow audience (e.g., beginner lifters, not "everyone") to keep it understandable enough to defend at Expo (priority 3).
- Sources: [fitness data overload systematic review](https://zenodo.org/records/17215832), [Oura "useless number" complaint thread](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/1874/oura-data-on-cronometer-is-useless), [Oura connection flakiness thread](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/2584/oura-connection-flakey-and-incomplete)

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-body-awareness]]
- Existing products: Upright GO 2 (posture, gyroscope+accelerometer, reminder-based), Mindsight Breathing Buddha (breath pacing, no app, ~2 month battery), Spire Health Tag (clip-on breath/stress tracker), Flowtime by Entertech (brain+body tracking with lessons), CoreCoach (pressure-based core feedback). Prices range roughly $30-300.
- Common complaints: Upright GO reviewed as having "temperamental tracking," and takes longer than its advertised 14 days to actually change habits — it's a passive reminder, not a trainer. General biofeedback devices need finger-placement/sensor fussing to get reliable readings. Notably, Spire's original "Stone" wearable was discontinued — a signal that this hardware-for-behavior-change space is hard to sustain as a business, though that's a company-longevity risk, not a proof the *product idea* is bad.
- The gap / opportunity: almost everything on the market is single-signal AND passively reminder-based ("you're slouching" / "you're stressed") rather than actively teaching the self-regulation skill in the moment. Nobody's closing the loop with real-time *guided correction* (e.g., haptic-cued breathing pattern, not just a buzz that says "fix it yourself"). Scoping to one signal — breath is probably the most tractable sensor-wise (chest/abdomen strap or clip) — is exactly the right instinct from her own note.
- Fit note vs. her goals: this is the strongest match of the five against all three priorities — genuinely personal (✅ priority 1, ties to [[nervous-system-regulation]]), real and underserved gap confirmed by research (✅ priority 2), and sensors/hardware+software depth for priority 3 — *if* she holds the line on one signal instead of trying to do posture+breath+stress at once.
- Sources: [Upright GO 2 review — TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/upright-go-2), [10 Best Biofeedback Devices 2026](https://outliyr.com/best-hrv-biofeedback-monitors), [Spire Stone review — OutdoorGearLab](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/reviews/fitness/pedometer/spire-stone), [Spire Health Tag — Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/fitness/a502165/review-spire-health-tag/)

### 2026-09-15 — [[idea-closet-organizer]]
- Existing products: Stylebook (manual entry, deep analytics, one-time purchase), Whering (AI-assisted, sustainability angle), Acloset (AI auto-tagging, claimed ~97% garment classification accuracy in 2026), Cladwell (capsule wardrobes), Indyx, Clueless (AI outfit planning). Big competitive signal: Google Photos is now rolling out its own free "Digital Closet" AI wardrobe-sorting feature built into a product people already use.
- Common complaints: manual cataloging is the #1 killer — Stylebook needs 2-3 hours of setup, and apps requiring heavy manual entry get abandoned. Whering specifically reviewed as "waaay too glitchy, to the point where it's actually unusable" by some users. Some apps drown in ads or go unmaintained. A few apps lean toward pushing *new* purchases rather than helping people use what they already own — which undercuts the actual premise.
- The gap / opportunity: cataloging friction, not outfit-decision logic, is what kills adoption — and computer vision is closing that gap fast, but even "good" AI cataloging implementations are still buggy in practice (Whering). Google Photos moving into this space natively is a real threat to any app that's *just* "photograph and catalog your clothes." A defensible angle would need something Google Photos can't trivially replicate — e.g., a physical capture station/hook that standardizes photos for clean cataloging, or leaning hard into the "decide what to wear" step instead of the cataloging step.
- Fit note vs. her goals: relatable and personal (✅ priority 1), real everyday friction (✅ priority 2) — but it's the most crowded space of the five, and now facing encroachment from a free, ubiquitous Google product, which raises real risk of feeling redundant or shallow by Expo unless she finds a sharp physical or workflow differentiator (priority 1 and 3 both at risk without one).
