# Idea: Closet Organization + Outfit Planning

A capstone idea for [[senior-capstone]]. **Problem:** people own a lot of clothing but
forget what they have, struggle to put outfits together, or buy new pieces without
considering what they already own. **Vision:** something that helps people see, use,
and combine the clothes they already have.

- **Target audience:** college students, young adults, people interested in fashion,
  people with large or disorganized closets.
- **Why it fits her:** UX/UI + possibly computer vision (cataloging clothes) + product
  thinking from [[capstone-skills]]. Could be software-leaning or software+physical.
- **Fit vs. [[capstone-goals]]:** relatable everyday problem (priority #2), fun/personal
  (priority #1). Open question: many closet/wardrobe apps exist — needs a distinctive
  angle; consider whether it leans on her fabrication strengths or is mostly software.

## Open questions for research (see [[capstone-research-log]])
- What do existing wardrobe/closet apps do, and why do people stop using them?
- Is the friction "cataloging" (computer vision could help) or "deciding what to wear"?
- Is there a physical/hardware component that would differentiate it?

## Research so far (2026-09-15)
Full findings: [[capstone-research-log]]. Key finding: cataloging friction (not
outfit-decision logic) is what actually kills these apps — manual entry takes hours and
people quit (Whering reviewed as "unusable"/glitchy even with AI auto-tagging). Bigger
problem: **Google Photos is rolling out its own free "Digital Closet" AI wardrobe
sorter** built into a product people already have open. My honest take: this is the
most crowded idea of the five, and now facing a free giant-tech competitor doing the
core feature — she'd need a sharp physical or workflow differentiator (like a capture
station, or focusing on the "what to wear" decision instead of cataloging) for it to
not feel redundant by Expo.
