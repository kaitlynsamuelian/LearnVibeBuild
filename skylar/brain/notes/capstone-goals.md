# Capstone Goals & Priorities

Kaitlyn's own goals and priorities for her [[senior-capstone]] (from her Goals &
Priorities assignment, 2026-09-15). These are the lens Skylar should use when helping
her evaluate ideas and make decisions.

## Her top priorities (ranked, in her words)
1. **Create a capstone she's genuinely proud of.** Wants to be excited for Expo,
   confident talking about it, and to have her "ducks in a row" so the process feels
   exciting and enjoyable rather than stressful or rushed. Stay organized, pick
   something she's genuinely interested in, end up with something that reflects her
   effort.
2. **Solve a real problem / design for a real need.** Clear purpose — solve an everyday
   problem, improve an existing experience, or make something useful/meaningful for a
   specific group. Not building something just because the tech is interesting.
3. **Really understand what she's building.** Leave with stronger skills and a deep
   understanding of the tools, tech, research, and design decisions — so she can
   confidently explain *why* she made the choices she did.

## Full goal list
- Create something she's proud of — polished, functional, portfolio-worthy, worth
  continuing after capstone.
- Solve/investigate real user problems — meaningful over merely technically interesting.
- Follow a strong process — realistic milestones, consistent progress, iterate instead
  of rush, leave time for polish.
- Understand what she's building — confidently explain not just *what* but *why* and *how*.

## Refined priorities & success (Sep 2026 worksheet)
Her own definition of success: creating something she's **genuinely proud of** (more than
grades/requirements) — meaningful and useful (solves a problem, improves or creates an
experience). She wants to look back and understand *why* she made her choices and what
value it provides. It should be something she's passionate about, that **grows or
strengthens her skills** (learn something new or level up an existing skill), and that
feels like "I *get* to do this" rather than "I *have* to." Core values: quality of work,
working hard, and a strong understanding of what she's doing — able to explain the tools,
ideas, and processes and take pride in a result that shows the effort.

**#1, above everything: make something she's TRULY PROUD OF.** Meaningful, useful, something
she's genuinely passionate about and understands deeply. This is the top filter — an idea
that nails pride/passion/meaning beats one that merely checks other boxes.

Her other priorities (supporting the above):
- ★ Solve a **real, validated** problem / create a meaningful experience (not tech for
  tech's sake).
- ★ **User-centered research + usability testing** ("I learned X, so I designed Y, tested
  it, found Z, and changed it").
- ★ **Deeper technical/design understanding** — learn ≥1 new skill/tech and understand it
  well enough to explain how + why it works.
- ★ **Grow her skills.** Physical prototyping / product design is ONE appealing growth
  area she's interested in — but it is **NOT required.** A software, digital, AI,
  interactive, or experiential project is completely valid if she'd be proud of it. Don't
  penalize non-physical ideas.

**Important:** the project does **not** have to be physical. Medium is open (software,
hardware, digital experience, installation, game, wearable, etc.). Pick whatever lets her
build something she's proud of and passionate about.

## How Skylar should use this
When weighing any idea (see [[senior-capstone]]), lead with: **would she be genuinely
proud of this and excited to work on it?** Then: real/meaningful need? good user-research
potential? does she learn + deeply understand new tech? Plus doable in a semester without
rushing and genuinely interesting to her. Treat physical prototyping as a bonus, not a
gate — judge software/digital ideas on equal footing.
