# Idea: Wall-Mount Collapsible Drying Rack

A capstone idea for [[senior-capstone]]. **Problem:** air-drying clothes eats valuable
floor space, racks are awkward to set up/store, and current options don't work well in
small spaces. **Vision:** an easy-to-use, removable, expandable drying rack that
attaches to the wall — collapsible, cheap, easy, maybe with multiple installation
methods.

- **Target audience:** college students, renters, apartment/dorm residents, people
  without dryers or who prefer to air-dry.
- **Why it fits her:** strong match for [[capstone-skills]] — physical prototyping,
  fabrication, product/industrial design. Very tangible, "make a real product" energy.
- **Fit vs. [[capstone-goals]]:** clearly a real everyday problem (priority #2) and
  portfolio-worthy/proud-of-able (priority #1). Open question: is it "deep" enough to
  fully satisfy priority #3 / feel substantial?

## Open questions for research (see [[capstone-research-log]])
- What wall-mount + collapsible drying racks already exist, and what do people complain about?
- Mechanisms for collapsible/expandable + removable wall mounting (without damaging walls).
- Materials, load capacity, price points people will pay.

## Research so far (2026-09-15)
Full findings: [[capstone-research-log]]. Key finding: this category is mature, cheap
($20-40), and well-reviewed (4.6/5 stars typical) — but every rack rated for real
laundry loads (60-80 lb) assumes screws into studs. True no-drill/renter-safe mounts
(Command strips, adhesive rails) cap around 15-40 lb, well under a wet load. **The gap
is a genuinely damage-free mount that's actually rated for real wet-laundry weight** —
a scoped, honest mechanical-engineering problem, not a reskin. My take: solid but not
the deepest of the five unless she commits hard to solving that specific mount problem —
otherwise it risks feeling like "a nicer version of a thing that already exists."
