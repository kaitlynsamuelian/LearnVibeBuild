Observational protocol, opened 2026-09-30. Aidan asked for the next six loads, documented. The point is not to test a concept. The point is to find out whether the reconfiguration we imagined matches what actually happens when nobody is trying to prove anything.

The rule

Hang normally. Do not improve your process because it is being recorded. Do not set up a nicer layout for the photo. If you do something inefficient, that is the data.

Before the first load

  • Photograph the rack empty, in the place it normally lives, from the same spot you will use every time.
  • Write down the rack you own: brand if known, number of bars, tiers, wings, whether any part is adjustable.
  • Note whether the rack is normally stored or normally out.
  • Pick the fixed camera spot and keep it. Comparable photos are the whole reason this works.

Photos to take every load

  • The wet clothes wherever they sit before hanging starts. Basket, floor, washer drum, bed.
  • The rack empty, from the fixed spot.
  • The rack fully loaded, from the fixed spot.
  • The rack fully loaded, from one side, so bar spacing and overlap are visible.
  • Any one item you had to fight with, close up.
  • The rack at the moment you take the last item off.
  • Anything that ended up somewhere that is not the rack. Curtain rod, chair, door, shower bar.

Inventory categories

Count items per load in these categories. Add a category if a load needs one, do not force an item into the wrong bucket.

  • Shirts and tops
  • Pants, jeans, leggings
  • Dresses and skirts
  • Bras
  • Underwear
  • Socks
  • Towels
  • Delicates and knits
  • Activewear
  • Bedding
  • Other

How to record each item type

For each category present in the load, write one line: where it went, how it was oriented, and why that spot. The “why” is the part that matters. Draped over two bars because it was heavy. Bottom tier because it was small. Hung on the end so the legs could fall. Clipped because there was nothing to drape it on. If the answer is “no reason, it was just next,” write that. That is a real finding.

What decides what

If the six loads show real, repeated wanting of a different frame shape, the reconfiguration track survives. If they show the same placement habit every time plus repeated poking at damp spots, that is a vote for the airflow track. If they show neither, the honest answer is that the problem is footprint and hours, and we go back to the question. See chopra-feedback.html and reconfiguration-skepticism.html.
