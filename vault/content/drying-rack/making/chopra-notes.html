How I got here

I was honestly very lost about what I wanted to do. I started from problems in my own life.

The drying rack is a constant source of tension with my roommates. It takes up a lot of space. They also break easily.

My mom has a drying rack that pulls out from the wall and folds back into it. Someone had to install that for her. I wanted an affordable renter and college-student version of that: people in dorms or rentals, people with small places who do not want a rack eating the floor, who could put it on the wall easily and take it off easily, with no permanent damage.

Then I talked to classmates. What they wished standard floor racks did is be modular. If you only have pants to dry, you could hang more pants. If you are only drying sports bras, you could arrange the rack for that instead. Same rack, set up for what you actually have.

That left me with two threads.

  • Installation. How and where it lives in the room. Floor, wall, door, and so on. On and off without wrecking the wall.
  • Configuration. How the rack itself is arranged for the laundry you have that day.
How can one drying system adapt to different laundry loads and living spaces, instead of requiring several different racks?

What already exists

What I have actually looked up so far. Not interviews. Not my own load numbers.

Close to what my mom has

Brabantia WallFix. Folds out from the wall, folds back. That is the stored-versus-open idea I grew up around. What it is missing for the people I care about: it wants a solid wall someone installs into, not typical rental drywall, and it is not a cheap dorm object you take with you.

Close to renter-safe

Polder Mountain Lock. Over the door, no drill, 40 lb official, about $35 to $57. This is the one that corrected me. Renter-safe plus a wet load already exists. What it is missing: it only lives on a door. If you need the door, or the next place has a bad door, you buy something else.

Other door racks are weaker (BLACK+DECKER 11 lb, Brabantia over-door 17 lb, BAKON hooks about 20 lb each). Still the door is the structure.

Close to “just put it on the wall”

High-capacity wall accordions (Woolite / ZMBESUP-class) hold a real load and fold flat. They need screws into a wall that can take it. The 60 lb number people repeat is a seller claim, not an official Woolite spec.

True adhesive is not that wall. 3M Command Large strips: 15 lb for a flat picture, not a cantilevered wet rack.

Close to a system with one core

IKEA SKÅDIS. One board, many attachments, one interface. Good logic. Wrong load. It is for light stuff, usually on a wall you are allowed to put up.

The floor racks people already own

Cheap, take the room, tip and break, which is the roommate fight I already have. Nothing I have found lets you rearrange that frame for pants one day and sports bras the next, as a product idea. You just live with the footprint.

So the split I keep seeing: high capacity usually means drill. No drill usually means the door. The version I first wanted (easy on the wall, easy off, no permanent damage, wet laundry) is the hole. Combining that with a rack you can reconfigure for the load is the other hole. I do not know yet if those belong in one project or two.
