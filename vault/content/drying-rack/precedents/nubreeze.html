NuBreeze Cool Air Drying System, by Viatek. Sold in Ireland by Expert.ie at €69.99 as model 1075, in the UK via High Street TV, and on Amazon as B081S8SD7P. A fan in the base pushes unheated room air out through curved flaps into the rack volume. Two speeds. No heating element. Retrieved 2026-09-30.

Seller claims, flagged as claims: “dries all your clothes up to 50% faster than drip drying alone,” “Two air speeds up to 5,000 metres per hour,” “Holds up to 45lbs (20kg) of laundry.” Listings disagree on drying length, one saying over 40ft (12m) and others over 32ft for the same model, so do not quote a figure without saying which listing it came from. NOT FOUND: wattage, decibel rating.

What it proves

An active-airflow drying rack is a shipping consumer product, not a concept. Somebody already decided that a fan is the feature worth building a rack around, and priced it at roughly a third of a heated airer.

It also proves the claim structure we would inherit. “Up to 50% faster than drip drying alone” is measured against doing nothing, which is the easy comparison. Note how carefully it avoids comparing itself to a room fan.

What it does not do

The air goes into the general volume around the load, not through the bars the clothes are hanging on. So it has the same blind spot as every other rack: the surface pressed against a rail, and the item shielded behind another item, are the last things the air reaches.

It is still a full floor footprint. Customer reviews surfaced in search for the Amazon listing include complaints that the fan is noisy for an apartment and, separately, that the fan is too weak. We read those as search summaries rather than on the page itself, so treat the wording as paraphrase. Either way, both complaints point at the same unsolved problem: without a designed air path you have to choose between loud and ineffective.

Link to our concept

This is the product that defines what “new” has to mean for us. Putting a fan on a rack is taken. The open ground is where the air is delivered: rod geometry, hole pattern, and reaching the contact surface. If our prototype cannot beat this kind of blow-air-at-the-pile approach, the airflow track is not differentiated.
