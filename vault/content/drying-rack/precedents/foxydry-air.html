Foxydry Air, and its bigger sibling Foxydry Pro. Italian ceiling-mounted motorised airers with integrated fans. The Air is listed at $599.70 (against $649.70) with “two air circulation fans” at 44W each, claimed capacity up to 35 kg, aluminium and stainless steel, lowering up to 70.86 in, four ceiling anchor points. The Pro adds a 750W heated-air function, “Warm airflow when your laundry needs extra support,” which “switches off automatically after three hours,” plus a remote and a 16W LED panel. Retrieved 2026-09-30.

Foxydry’s marketing mentions “speeding up the drying times” through “recycling of hot air” but publishes no drying-time number. There is nothing here to quote as performance. No decibel spec published.

What it proves

The premium, engineered, airflow-first drying system Aidan described already exists at the top of the market, at $600 and up. Fans plus optional warm air plus motors plus chosen materials, sold as a system rather than a rack. So the positioning is viable, and the price ceiling is much higher than we assumed.

It also proves the cleanest solution to the footprint problem: go to the ceiling and the rack stops competing for the room. That is a real answer to living in laundry mode, and it is not ours.

What it does not do

It needs four anchor points in a ceiling. That is a landlord conversation, a drill, and a structural assumption, which is the exact opposite of the renter-safe constraint the earlier research pass established. This is a homeowner product.

The fans circulate air around the load. They do not deliver air through the rods to the contact surface, so the uneven-drying complaint is not addressed by design, only diluted by more air.

It is also the wrong scale for a student capstone. We cannot outspend a motor, a heated-air module and an LED panel, and we should not try. Note this is a different product from the existing foxydry card in this shelf, which is the Duo railing clamp.

This is the ceiling of the category and the proof that airflow can be the premium feature. Use it to justify the direction, never to justify the specification. Our version has to win on the air path and on not needing a ceiling, because those are the two things Foxydry leaves open.
