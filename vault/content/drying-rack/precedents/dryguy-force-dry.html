DryGuy Force Dry DX boot and garment dryer. A rotary fan pushes gently warmed air up through hollow posts and into the inside of boots, gloves and liners. Listings state 120V, 240 watts, heats to 105 °F / 40.5 °C, a heat/no-heat switch, a 3-hour timer, two removable extension tubes for boots up to 16 inches, dries up to four items at once, about 3.25 lbs. Manufacturer claim, flagged: dry time “Approximately 1-2 Hours.” Retrieved 2026-09-30 from https://dryguy.com/products/force-dry-dx and retail listings.

What it proves

Air delivered through the structure into the garment is a real, shipping, unremarkable product. Not a patent, not a concept render. You can buy it at a hardware store.

It also proves the temperature answer. It runs at 105 °F, barely above body temperature, with a switch to turn the heat off entirely, and it is sold on the promise that it will not shrink, warp or damage custom-fit liners. That is the model for anything we do with warmth later: low, controlled, optional, fabric-safe. It is the opposite of the tumble-dryer approach and the opposite of the heated airers that get recalled.

And the form is the mechanism. The posts exist to be an air path first and a holder second. That is exactly the inversion Aidan was describing for a rack.

What it does not do

It is built for one garment shape, an enclosed cavity. A boot traps the air you push into it. A hanging shirt does not, so a design that works beautifully for footwear tells us nothing about whether air stays anywhere near a draped t-shirt. That is the single biggest open question in our experiment.

It handles four items. A laundry load is dozens. Scaling the air supply from four posts to a full rack is not a detail, it is the engineering problem.

Its dry-time claim is for footwear and must never be reused as a laundry number.

Link to our concept

This is the mechanism precedent for the airflow track, and the safety precedent for how to introduce warmth if we ever do. Use it to argue that air-through-structure is a solved idea in an adjacent category. Do not use it to argue that it will work on a hanging garment, because that is the part we have to find out.
