Kinds of flowers

A small gallery. Scroll and each screen is one flower with a bit about it. No encyclopedia. Just types you would actually recognize.

Scroll ↓

Garden classic

Rose

Rosa

The one people mean when they say flower. Tight spiral of petals, thorns on the stem, smells like a whole industry got built around it. Hundreds of kinds, from little shrub roses to the long-stem ones in grocery bunches.

Follows the sun

Sunflower

Helianthus annuus

A big yellow disc with a seed-packed center. Young heads track the sun during the day. Grown for oil, snacks, and that one photo everyone takes in a field. Tall, obvious, hard to mix up with anything else.

Scent first

Lavender

Lavandula

Spikes of tiny purple flowers on silver-green stems. People grow it for smell, tea, and those dry bunches that stay in a kitchen for a year. Likes sun and does not want to sit in wet soil.

Short season, huge bloom

Peony

Paeonia

Round, packed, almost too many petals. They show up hard in late spring and then they are gone. Ants on the buds are normal. Heavy heads that flop if you do not stake them.

Cup on a stick

Tulip

Tulipa

A simple cup of petals on a straight stem. Planted as bulbs, famous in spring and in Dutch still-lifes. Comes in almost every color except a true blue. They keep growing in the vase, which is why the stems curve.

Paper petals

Poppy

Papaver

Thin, creased petals that look like tissue. A dark center. California poppies are orange and low. Breadseed poppies are the big red ones. They self-sow if you let the seed heads stand.

Trumpet, spots, pollen

Lily

Lilium

Six petals (technically tepals), often spotted, with dusty pollen that stains a tablecloth. Asiatic lilies are upright. Orientals are the fragrant ones. Not the same plant as a daylily, even if the name sounds close.

A mop of little flowers

Hydrangea

Hydrangea

What looks like one giant bloom is a cluster of smaller ones. Color can shift with the soil (more acid, more blue on some types). Big, round, a little old-fashioned in the best way. They drink a lot of water in a vase.
