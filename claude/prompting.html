Chapter 5

Prompting Tips

A "prompt" is just your message to Claude. You don't need special words — you need clear ones. These habits turn okay answers into great ones.

Six habits that make Claude shine

1

Give context up front

Tell Claude who it's for and why. "Explain compound interest to a 12-year-old" beats "explain compound interest." Context shapes everything.

2

Be specific about the outcome

Say what a good result looks like: length, tone, audience, format. "A friendly 100-word summary with 3 bullet points" gives Claude a clear target.

3

Show an example

If you have a style in mind, paste a short example. Claude is great at matching a pattern you show it — "write more like this."

4

Ask for a format

Want a table, a checklist, or steps? Just ask. "Put it in a table with columns for pros and cons" gets you exactly that.

5

Let it think for hard problems

For tricky questions, add "think it through step by step." Claude reasons more carefully and you can follow the logic.

6

Iterate — it's a conversation

Not quite right? Say "shorter," "more formal," "focus on the second point." You rarely nail it in one shot, and that's fine.

Weak prompt vs. strong prompt

Same goal, very different results. The strong versions add context, specifics, and a format.

✗ Vague

"write about dogs"

Claude has to guess the length, angle, and audience.

✓ Specific

"Write a warm 150-word intro for a first-time dog owner's guide, in a friendly tone, ending with one reassuring sentence."

✗ Vague

"help with my budget"

No numbers, no goal — nothing to work with.

✓ Specific

"Here's my monthly income and expenses [paste]. Suggest a simple budget that saves 15%, as a table, and flag my two biggest costs."

Prompting Claude Code (for building)

The same habits apply, plus a few coding-specific ones.

Work in small steps. Ask for one change, review it, then continue. Easier to check and undo than one giant request.

Ask for a plan first. For anything big: "outline your plan before you change any files." You approve the approach, then it builds.

Paste the exact error. The full error text usually lets Claude pinpoint the fix immediately.

Save preferences in CLAUDE.md. Recurring instructions belong there so you don't repeat them each session.

Copy-and-tweak starter prompts

Steal these and adjust to your situation.

"Explain [topic] to me like I'm completely new, with one everyday analogy."

"Summarize this document in 5 bullet points, then list any action items." (attach a file)

"Draft three subject-line options for this email, from formal to casual."

"Review my writing for clarity and tone. Suggest edits, but keep my voice."

"Outline a plan before changing any files, then wait for my approval." (Claude Code)

"Here's an error I got: [paste]. Find the cause and propose a fix." (Claude Code)
