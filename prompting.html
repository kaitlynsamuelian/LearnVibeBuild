Chapter 5

Prompting Tips

A "prompt" is just the message you send the AI. You don't need magic words — you need clear ones. These habits are the difference between "meh" and "wow, that's exactly what I wanted."

The five habits that matter most

1

Be specific about the outcome

Say what you want to end up with, not just the topic. "Make a button" is vague; "Add a blue Sign Up button under the heading that links to signup.html" gives the AI a target.

2

Give context with @

Point the AI at the right files using the @ menu. Showing it the relevant file beats making it guess. Context is the number-one driver of good results.

3

Work in small steps

Ask for one change at a time, review it, then continue. Small steps are easier to check and easier to undo than one giant request.

4

Share the full error

Something broke? Paste the exact error message. The AI can usually pinpoint the fix when it can see the real text, instead of "it doesn't work."

5

Iterate — it's a conversation

Not quite right? Just say so: "close, but make it bigger" or "do that but without changing the colors." The AI remembers the conversation.

6

Ask it to explain

Don't understand a change? Ask "explain what you just did in simple terms." Cursor is a tutor as much as a builder — use it to learn.

Weak prompt vs. strong prompt

Same goal, very different results. Notice how the stronger versions add specifics and context.

✗ Vague

"make my site look better"

The AI has to guess what "better" means and where.

✓ Specific

"In @index.html, add more spacing between sections, use a dark background, and make the heading font larger and bolder."

✗ Vague

"it's broken, fix it"

No error, no file — the AI is working blind.

✓ Specific

"When I click Submit I get this error: [paste error]. The form is in @contact.js. Can you find and fix the cause?"

A healthy mindset for working with AI

A few attitudes that keep you safe, effective, and learning.

Review everything. The AI is fast and helpful, but not perfect. Skim its changes before accepting — you're the final editor.

It's okay to not understand yet. Ask "why?" freely. Every explanation you request is a mini lesson.

Save your work. Learn basic Git or keep backups. Checkpoints help, but they're not a substitute for real version control.

Copy-and-tweak starter prompts

Stuck on how to phrase something? Steal these and adjust to your situation.

"Explain what this project does, file by file, in plain English."

"Add a simple contact form to @index.html with name, email, and message fields."

"There's a bug: [describe what happens]. Investigate and propose a fix before changing anything."

"Make this page responsive so it looks good on phones. Show me the changes first."

"What does the word '[term]' mean in this code? Explain like I'm brand new."

"Add comments to this file so a beginner could understand it."
