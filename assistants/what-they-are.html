Chapter 1

What a “personal assistant” actually is

People mash three different products into one word. If you mix them up, you’ll install a coding agent and wonder why it doesn’t text you about lunch.

Three layers people confuse

Chat

You open a website. It replies. ChatGPT, claude.ai, Gemini. No inbox. No calendar unless you paste it in.

Coding agent

Lives in an editor or terminal. Edits your repo. Cursor and Claude Code are this. Great at files. Bad at “text me on Telegram.”

Personal assistant

Supposed to know you, sit in a chat app, and take actions over time. Muse, Hermes, OpenClaw, some Telegram bots. Memory + tools + a channel.

Hosted vs you-run-it

Hosted

Muse (Meta). Some Telegram products (folk, Overfolder, and friends). Easy install: an app or a username. Their cloud. Their uptime. They can see what you type unless they promise otherwise — read the privacy page, don’t trust the ad.

Self-hosted

Hermes Agent (Nous Research) and OpenClaw. A process on your laptop or a cheap VPS. You pick the model (often still a paid API). Close the lid, a laptop-only agent goes to sleep.

Open software ≠ private chat. Hermes and OpenClaw are open-source apps. If you point them at Claude or GPT, those companies still see the prompts. Same idea as the open-source guide.

What “doing the work” really means

Marketing says “it books the flight.” What that usually is:

  1. A model (the brain) — Claude, GPT, Muse Spark, a local Llama…
  2. A gateway — the always-on program that holds sessions, tools, and chat connections.
  3. A channel — Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, iMessage, a custom app.
  4. Permissions — can it only read, or also send / pay / run a terminal command?

You can swap brains. You should be stingy with permissions. The channel is just where you talk.
